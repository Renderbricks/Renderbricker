# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The project follows
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/): PATCH for fixes, MINOR for new features, MAJOR for
changes that break existing scenes or settings. Only changes to the add-on itself are listed;
documentation changes are not.

## [1.3.0] – unreleased

- **Large parts no longer run out of memory.** A part of more than 40,000 faces – a baseplate, a large hull – is now converted piece by piece. The 48 x 48 baseplate needed about 58 GB and stopped with *not enough free memory*; it now takes about 6 GB and half a minute. Pieces of the same shape – the 2,304 studs of that plate – are converted once and put in place, with their own stud numbers. The result is the same as converting the part in one go. The progress bar moves within such a part and says which piece it is at. If a single piece is still too large for the free memory, Apply stops with that message and leaves the part as imported, instead of letting Blender crash. A baseplate imported with Renderbricks' *Studs for Random Walk* is one connected piece of a million faces; it is now converted stud field by stud field, each kind of field once – the 48 x 48 baseplate in about five minutes and 10.5 GB, where it stopped at 56 GB before. The fields meet without a visible seam (their shape differs from converting the plate in one go by at most 0.01 mm).
- **Each piece of a part is smoothed on its own.** How much a round shape is widened against the shrinking, and what is taken back where a face would fold, is now decided for each piece of a part separately – before, one folding face anywhere took the widening off every stud of a plate. With *Edges: Bevel* the rounding of an edge no longer reaches into a neighbouring piece (a stud beside the edge of a plate).
- **Textures at seams:** a point exactly on the seam of a texture could take the texture of the other side (a streak along a wrapped texture); and a few points that should have been put back to their place on the model stayed where the smoothing left them.
- **Edges: Shader or Bevel** – a new switch below the levels. *Shader* is what Renderbricker did so far: the importer's material rounds the edges, with a Bevel node in Cycles and a baked map in EEVEE. With *Bevel*, **Apply** gives the part's own sharp edges a real bevel in the smooth copies, after the subdivision: the edge is rounded in the geometry, so the outline is round too – in profile, against the sky, in close-ups. Every edge gets the same width, the Radius, as Blender's Bevel modifier does with the same width; at a corner where three edges meet the rounding closes like a ball. The edges look the same in Cycles and EEVEE, also on parts that have no map, and close-ups show a clean line of light where the shader's highlights are blotchy. Where a detail is smaller than the bevel, the bevel stays flatter there instead of folding the faces. Studs, logos and modelled chamfers keep the rounding Mecabricks gave them; the material's own rounding is switched off for the bevelled copies by itself. Costs faces for the extra edges: 11 % more over the whole Mecabricks library at render level 2, 19 % on a typical part (4,776 parts measured). Changing the switch makes Apply convert the parts again.
- **When Edges, Radius or Segments change, the panel says so:** a red note below them – the parts still show the old edges – and a red **Apply – edges changed**; with *Shader* a changed Radius only needs Apply to reach the materials. Rendering before Apply shows a warning in the status bar, and **Save render scene** says it in its question.
- **Bevel shader too** (only with *Bevel*, off by default, for testing): the importer's bevel shader rounds the edges on top of the real bevel, at the Radius – switches at once, no Apply.
- **Segments** (1 to 3, start 2), only with *Bevel*: the facets of the bevel across an edge – 1 is a flat chamfer, 3 rounds the outline. The shading is round with any number.
- **Radius (mm)** (0.08 to 0.2 mm, start 0.1; 0.2 is close to the size the importer shows) is the edge radius: how far the rounding reaches on each face from the edge. It sets the rounding for both: the bevel's width, and in *Shader* the importer's Bevel value of the parts in scope (the value that looks that size). The start value holds for both, so after **Apply** the edges are finer than the importer shows by itself (0.1 instead of about 0.2 mm). **Remove** puts the importer's own value back.
- **Bevel maps for EEVEE** are only baked with *Edges: Shader* – a bevelled copy has its rounding in the mesh.
- **Independent add-on, said in the add-on too:** the tooltip of **Import from Mecabricks** and *About* now say that Renderbricker is an independent add-on, not an official Mecabricks product, and has no importer of its own – the button starts the Mecabricks add-on by Nicolas 'Scrubs' Jarraud, which must be installed.
- **Round shapes keep their size** (rules 3.10): the subdivision makes round surfaces a little thinner than the model – up to 0.16 mm on a cylinder. Where two pieces of a part only touch, such as a handle lying on a plate (reported on 3839v2), a dark gap opened between them. Whole cylinders – bars, studs, tubes, axles, the walls of holes – are now widened from their axis by exactly what they lose, and nothing else: flat faces, sharp edges, a cube, roundings along an edge, the round ends of a flat part and collars round a hole keep their shape and size. Because the rules changed, the next **Apply** converts every part again.
- **Small rounded corners come out round** (rules 3.9): where Mecabricks models a rounded corner with just two straight pieces, the smooth copy kept those two pieces – the corner stayed angular however high the level (reported on part 18747). Such corners are now rounded, up to a corner radius of about 0.6 mm; larger corners of this kind stay as they were. Where such a rounding ended in a hard edge of the model it kept a kink there; now it runs out softly on both sides – on the front and on the back of a plate alike. 234 of the 4,776 parts of the Mecabricks library change, by at most 0.07 mm. Because the rules changed, the next **Apply** converts every part again.

## [1.2.8] – 2026-10-11

- **Blender 4.5: no crash right after opening a file.** Blender 4.1 to 4.5 crash when a script removes a scene in the same call that opened a file; a script that opened a scene and ran Apply at once took Blender down, because Apply tidies up a helper scene. The add-on now removes its helper scenes a moment later in that case. Pressing Apply by hand was not affected; Blender 5.0 and later do not have the fault.
- **Bevel maps for EEVEE (optional):** a new option below the levels, off by default because it costs baking time – about a second per part during Apply. With the scope *Selected*, Apply bakes the maps of the selected parts only. EEVEE cannot round edges the way Cycles does; the importer shows a small map there that was made for the coarse import, and round rims come out scalloped. With the option on, **Apply** bakes the rounded edges of every converted part from its smooth copy into a new map of the same size, and the part uses it in EEVEE: round rims are clean arcs. Cycles is not changed. The import keeps its map (Subdivision OFF shows it as before), **Remove** takes the new maps out again. Works with the cache file, with several Blender processes, with **Convert headless** and **Save render scene**. For parts imported with their EEVEE maps (Renderbricks); other parts are left as they are.
- **No scope "All" any more:** the scope is **Collection** (new default) or **Selected**. *All* converted every model in the scene, also ones that were not meant. Scenes saved with *All* open with *Collection* and an empty list – their converted parts stay converted and render as before; Apply asks for a collection first.
- **Collection list:** **+** with a part selected adds the collection of that part – one part of the model is enough – instead of the collection active in the Outliner. With nothing selected, **+** offers the collections that hold parts. **−** with no entry selected asks which collection to take out; a new trash button empties the list. The Scene Collection can no longer end up in the list as an empty entry; parts lying directly in it get a hint (put them into a collection, or use *Selected*).
- **An imported model is in the collection list by itself**, whichever way it is imported – **Import from Mecabricks**, *File > Import* or the importer's own panel – so Apply works right after the import. A collection that arrives in the scene with mesh objects of its own is listed, and the line below the panel says so; a collection made from parts that were already there is left to **+**.
- **Every mesh is treated as a Mecabricks part:** the Start Guide, the tooltips and the reference now say plainly that Renderbricker does not tell LEGO parts from other meshes – keep your own models out of the listed collections.
- **F12 shows its picture also with "Keep User Interface":** with *Preferences > Interface > Render In* set to *Keep User Interface*, Blender opens nothing for a render and shows only the status bar. Renderbricker's renders (F12, Ctrl+F12, the Render buttons, *All views*) now open the render window then; the preference itself is not changed. An Image Editor that already shows the *Render Result* is used as it is.
- **The cache question right after Save As:** saving the scene under another name or in another folder now asks at once whether to move or copy the cache file, or to decide later. Before, only a box in the panel said so. The last step of the Start Guide shows that box too.
- **Convert headless says what it does:** tooltip, question, Start Guide, the console window and the reference carry the same sentence – it does the same as Apply outside Blender, writes a new scene file, leaves the open scene as it is and does not render.
- **Start Guide, first step:** one thing is highlighted at a time – *Import from Mecabricks* while no model is in the list, then *Save As*, then *Next*.
- **Tutorial** next to **Documentation** at the foot of the panel and in the last step of the Start Guide.
- The numbers after Apply and Check have half a line between their points, like the explanations of the Start Guide.
- **About:** a link to Instagram; the first link goes to vr.renderbricks.com.
- **Which importer is used:** below **Import from Mecabricks** the panel now names the importer – Mecabricks Advanced, Mecabricks Lite or Renderbricks – with its version, and the log file names it too. If two importers are enabled at the same time, the line turns red and says which one imports.
- **Start Guide easier to read:** the explanations of every step are set as justified text with hyphenation, like the text in *About* – even lines instead of a ragged right edge – with a small gap between the points.
- **Remove All:** a second button next to **Remove** takes every converted part of the file back to its import – all models, every scene, copies of parts deleted meanwhile – after a question. **Remove** works on the scope only. Before, the whole-file Remove was the scope *All*.
- **Save render scene:** a new button below *Convert headless* writes `<scene>_render.blend` next to the scene, for rendering on a computer without Renderbricker – a render farm, for example. Every converted part is at the render level, and the parts and their materials are inside the file: it needs neither the add-on nor the cache file. Before, a scene with a cache file opened there without materials and rendered the viewport level. The open scene, its file and its cache stay as they are; the render scene is a snapshot, so click again after changes. It is about as large as the cache file. Reported by the maintainer.
- **Only the scope is converted, also headless:** **Convert headless** now converts only the parts in scope – with *Selected* or *Collection* nothing outside is touched. Before, it converted every part of the file whatever the scope said. The list of parts is saved next to the start script (`<result>_objects.json`).
- **Rendering keeps parts outside the scope as they are:** a part outside the scope that uses the same mesh as a converted part (a copy made with Alt+D, for example) now renders as it shows in the viewport. Before, F12 rendered it subdivided. For scenes converted with an earlier version, F12 keeps the old behaviour at viewport level 0 until the next **Apply**.
- An object outside the scope that already shows a subdivided copy of a mesh converted again inside the scope gets the new copy, also with several Blender processes and the cache file.

## [1.2.7] – 2026-10-06

- **EEVEE closer to Cycles:** with the EEVEE button, rays may now hit back faces (Backface Hit, Blender 5.2 and newer) and screen tracing runs at full quality – arches, doorways and insides are no longer lit bluish-bright but dark as in Cycles. The sun lamp's shadow is no longer filtered, so its edges sit closer to Cycles' shadows. EEVEE renders take about 10 % longer. Settings that do not exist in older Blender versions are skipped.

## [1.2.6] – 2026-10-06

- **Editing a converted part:** select it and press **Tab** – its links switch to the imported mesh and Edit Mode starts on it; leaving Edit Mode converts it again and switches it back on (only this part). Before, Edit Mode was refused on parts from the cache file ("error changing modes"). With the cache file, saving the scene writes the edited parts into the cache by themselves (only them – the other copies are carried over); until then the panel names them under Apply (**Update cache** does it at once). A part edited with *Subdivision OFF* is converted again too when you leave Edit Mode. Reported on Discord.
- **Apply notices edited parts:** a part whose imported mesh was edited since its conversion is converted again – before, Apply kept the old copy.
- **Saving the first time continues:** on a scene not saved yet, Apply, Cache and Convert headless open *Save As* and continue by themselves once the scene is saved – no second click.
- **Zooming in close:** Apply and the render camera set the 3D views' *Clip Start* to 0.1 cm, so real-size parts are no longer cut when zooming in (the render camera gives the old value back when switched off).
- **Textures stay where they belong:** textures that follow the parts' UV maps – mould lines, the grainy slope texture, prints and logos – lie on the smoothed part where they lie on the import. The smoothing used to slide the faces along the part, and the texture slid with them: the mould line of the curved bar 7052 ended lower and with a changed corner, the grain of slope 3044 streaked along the rounded edge. Now the points slide back along the smooth surface to their import place; the shape stays the same. Also gone: a dent in EEVEE where two drawn lines meet (7052 at level 3). Both reported by the maintainer. Already converted scenes are converted again on the next **Apply**.
- **Remove clears the whole file** with the scope *All*: the parts in every scene go back to the import, and copies of parts deleted in the meantime no longer stay in the file and no longer keep the cache file linked. The summary names how many copies were deleted and, with *Selected* or *Collections*, how many stay outside the scope.
- **Render camera in ACES 2.0:** in Blender 5.0 and newer switching the render camera on sets the view ACES 2.0 with the reference gamut compression (Blender 4.5 keeps AgX), and the sun strength starts on 0.06 instead of 0.03 – ACES 2.0 renders darker (a value set by you stays). The summary notes when the file's working colour space is not ACEScg. Switching off brings back your view and look exactly – a look of your own (e.g. AgX Punchy) was lost before.
- **Folds at tiny faces:** a fold that only showed in the finished smoothed copy is now repaired too (one part in more than 1,300).

## [1.2.2] – 2026-10-04

- **EEVEE** on renders the 3D views with EEVEE (*Rendered*, the scene's own settings – the same as F12) instead of Material Preview.
- **EEVEE samples:** switching EEVEE on sets 128 samples for the viewport and 256 for rendering.
- **Curves on flat faces:** where a rounding meets a flat face in a coarse curve, the edge now follows the curve instead of staying a polyline with kinks – e.g. the curved slopes 29119 / 29120, whose curve met the 45° face and the flat side with corners (reported by a user). Designed kinks stay sharp.

## [1.2.0] – 2026-10-01

- **Cycles | EEVEE** right under the render camera button: two switches, both off at the start. **Cycles** on: the 3D views render with Cycles (Rendered) and F12 renders with Cycles – the physical sky with its sun disc lights the model. **EEVEE** on: the 3D views show EEVEE (Material Preview with the Renderbricks sky and its lamp) and F12 renders with EEVEE. Pressed again, the views go back to the shading they had. EEVEE takes the sky's sun only very weakly, so there the sky lights without its disc and a sun lamp "Renderbricks Sun" stands in for the sun: linked to the sun sliders (direction, over the top, "Sun turns with the camera", the mirrored sky of Bottom, direct edits of the Sky node), with the strength and colour the sun has in Cycles at that elevation and altitude – the model is as bright in EEVEE as in Cycles (before: about 60 %). EEVEE is set to everything it can do: ray tracing and global illumination at full resolution, soft shadows with the most rays and steps (in the viewport too), a sharp world probe.
- **Models at real size** (import scale 0.001, as the reworked Mecabricks importers use): EEVEE's shadow and ray-tracing sizes follow the scale of the parts, so shadows stay sharp instead of blotchy; when the scale is changed in the Mecabricks panel, the render camera moves with the model and the picture stays as it was.
- **Blender 4.5:** Cycles crashed when rendering with the Renderbricks sky, whose sky type (Multiple Scattering, Blender 5.x) 4.5 does not know – such a sky now becomes Nishita.

## [1.1.0] – 2026-09-30

- **Import from Mecabricks** at the top of the panel (and in step 1 of the Start Guide) – the same as File > Import > Mecabricks (.zmbx). Greyed out, with a note and a link to www.mecabricks.com, while neither Mecabricks Lite nor Advanced is enabled.
- **Sun sliders** in the render camera: Elevation (−15° to 195°, over the top to the other side), Rotation (0° to 360°), Altitude (0 to 100,000 m) and Strength of the sky (0.01 to 0.1), each with step arrows and a button back to the standard. Typed values stay as they are. With the view Bottom the mirrored sky follows a moment after the last change.
- **Sun in picture: on/off** below the sun sliders (default off): the sun disc lights the model as before – highlights, sharp shadows and reflections – but is not seen in the picture (Cycles; EEVEE never shows it).
- **New standard sky:** Strength 0.03 instead of 0.2.
- **Tidy world nodes:** the nodes of the Renderbricks sky (and of the mirrored sky of Bottom) are laid out in columns along the signal, without overlaps. A world with nodes of your own is left as it is.

## [1.0.1] – 2026-09-30

- No functional changes – the add-on's code is split into modules by task (panel, operators, Start Guide; the core as its own package), so it is easier to read and to extend.

## [1.0.0] – 2026-09-29

- **Smooth parts for rendering:** Apply sets creases on the parts of a Mecabricks import by a tested rule set – sharp where the real part is sharp, round where it is round – and bakes the subdivided result into one copy per part, shared by all its links. The imported meshes stay unchanged; Remove goes back to them.
- **Viewport and render level** separately; F12 and Ctrl+F12 render with the render level.
- **Scope:** All, Selected or chosen collections with their child collections.
- **Several Blender processes** convert large scenes in parallel, as many as the processor and the free memory allow; **Low Memory** (Auto, Off, On) for very large parts or computers with little memory.
- **Convert headless** converts the whole scene in a terminal and writes a new file; the open scene is not changed.
- **Cache file** next to the scene keeps the scene file small; Move or Copy the cache after Save As.
- **Check** finds folded faces and open seams; **Subdivision ON/OFF** compares with the import.
- **Render camera:** a camera, a sky and render settings of their own, six views each into its own render slot, All views, sun fixed or turning, Transparent, Resolution Scale and sample presets – your own camera, world and settings come back when it is switched off.
- **Start Guide** leads through the workflow inside Blender; optional **log file** next to the scene.
- Blender 4.5 LTS or newer on Windows, Linux and macOS.
