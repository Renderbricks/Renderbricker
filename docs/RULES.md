# Mecabricks → Subdivision Surface: Rule Set

Version 3.16 · 2026-11-15 (3.1: W15, 3.2: W5b, 3.3: W16, 3.4: W14 Keep Corners, W10b, 3.5: W14b texture-aware UVs, 3.6: W14c reparametrised copies replace W14b, 3.7: W17 coarse round­ings, 3.8: W18 their ends, 3.9: W17 on the outline of flat faces, 3.10: W19 shrink com­pen­sa­tion, 3.11: W20 per island, batches, 3.14: W2 wedge excep­tion on mechan­i­cal parts only, 3.15: W2b an arc's last step, S1 the first rule for a single part, 3.16: W21 – W29 the shrink com­pen­sa­tion of every round­ing and pieces that touch stay closed, W14c keeps closed out­lines, W25 removed) · imple­mented in `addon/renderbricker/rbcore/` (`creases.py`, `welding.py`: `process`, weld; 3.16: `cagegeom.py`, `shrink.py`, `outlines.py`, `contacts.py`, `cover.py`, `reparam.py`; the island rules up to 2.4 remain as `process_24`, `--method rules24`), checked by `scripts/core/verify_part.py` and `scripts/core/verify_generic.py`
Every rule names the part and the devel­op­ment run in which it was found; the run records are kept with the project, not in this repository.

The rules make imported Mecabricks parts sub­di­vid­able (Catmull-Clark) without cracks, folds or lost detail. Each rule comes from a con­crete finding; the column *Origin* names the part and run where it was found.

## W · Rules 3.0: weld (runs 48–50, refined 53–56)

The island rules (sec­tions 2–4) treat every Mecabricks island as an open surface and hold the
seams together with pins – pins that keep sil­hou­ettes angular (tail of 10509a4, nostril). Rules
3.0 weld the seams in a tem­po­rary copy instead; the orig­i­nal mesh gets nothing (no attributes).

| No. | Rule | Why | Source |

| W1 | **Weld** coincident open vertices along seams (open edges whose end points coincide with those of another open edge), in a temporary copy. Never two corners of one face, never two corners of one face onto one target, no point contacts; faces that would become duplicates stay unwelded. | Seams become ordinary edges: no seam gaps, creased edge chains smooth as curves. | runs 48, 50 (NINJAGO lost corners, 1,472 → 1,448 loops) |

Nearly tangent seams (open seams through one surface) stay smooth. Edges with more than two faces: creased. Logo islands: inner edges above 50° (as R-logo). | Mecabricks splits at hard edges; tangent splits are no edges. Hair 13768: a 29° seam made a third crease and so a corner at the face opening (P18). | W3 | **Flat stays flat:** a flat island without round outline gets all its inner edges creased (as R2). | Left free, open edges of flat faces curled inward (29119 0.14). | run 50 |
| W4 | **Corners:** on a crease chain (creased and open edges) a vertex with two chain edges turning > 30° that is not on a circle/arc (K3/K3b applied to the chain) gets a vertex crease; three creased edges are a corner anyway. Sharp corners of the original island outlines stay pinned – except the tips of smooth wedges (as W2: turn ≥ 150°, partners bend < 30°); a wedge tip where a partner island itself turns sharply (Technic ends) stays pinned. **Organic shapes** (every face at the vertex in a curved island): a bend counts as a corner from 60°; a hairpin turn (≥ 150°) is the end of a groove or strand and stays free. | 54200: a corner where three islands meet almost tangentially lost all creases, the surface curled 0.29. | W5 | **Ring:** ≥ 6 flat patches around a recess or hole (walls facing the axis), meeting at equal angles (±1°) with one angle to a common axis = polygonised cylinder/cone: wall seams smooth, smooth normals on that island. | W12 | **Steep inner edges:** an edge inside one island (not a former seam) whose faces meet at ≥ 85° is creased (INNER_SHARP). | Mecabricks sometimes leaves a right angle inside an island with normals smooth across it; left free, subdivision rounds it off: 35787 recess corner 1.39, NINJAGO 2450 rim 1.56, tyre 15413 0.64 → 0.08 / 0.28 / 0.37. | W13 | **Plane beside a bevel:** Mecabricks models rounded edges as a strip of narrow faces in the same island as the large plane faces beside them. Within an island, planar patches (normals within 1°) are found; an edge between two patches bending ≥ 10° is creased when one patch is at least 8× the area of the other (PLANE_RATIO). | Left free, the subdivision pulled the rounding far into the planes – bevels grew, the part shrank (user, Ratatouille 86209). With W13 the bevel rounds within its own width. Deviation better in 344 meshes, worse in none (8 models); horse and hairs without hard lines. Fallback: faces that still fold after the repair (W10) release the W13 edges within twice their size and the part is welded again (Bugatti 35188: 4 folds → 0). | run 58 |
| W14 | **UVs smoothed, corners kept:** the Subdivision modifier uses `uv_smooth = 'PRESERVE_CORNERS_JUNCTIONS_AND_CONCAVE'` (Blender's "Keep Corners, Junctions, Concave"; up to rules 3.3 `'PRESERVE_BOUNDARIES'`, "Keep Boundaries"). | The subdivision also moves vertices *within* a plane; smoothed UVs move along, so prints keep their shape. Tested on 86209 (texture as emission): linear UVs (`'NONE'`, add-on 1.6.7/1.6.8) and UVs projected from the original triangles distort the lettering; all smoothing modes keep it. The wavy print lines the user saw came from the spreading bevel and are gone with W13. Rules 3.4 (user report, 3044v2): with Keep Boundaries the grainy slope texture streaked along the rounded edges, where points of the slope face move into the rounding. Measured per child face against its original face's UV map (share distorted > 1.5×): 3044v2 10.6 % → 4.3 %, Bricks-Sloping (85 parts) uvmap0 9.0 → 7.0 %, uvmap1/2 10.9 → 8.5 %; prints of 86209 more exact (worst 1.42 → 1.08), its uvmap0 1.9 → 2.5 %. UVs projected from the import within the UV island: no visible gain over the mode. | runs 58, 212 |
| W14c | **Reparametrised copies** (replace W14b of 3.5): the copies are baked with linear UVs (uv_smooth NONE), and every vertex slides along the smooth surface to the point nearest to its *parametric* point on the import – the import position carried through the subdivision as a corner attribute, interpolated like the linear UVs. Slides below 0.2 % of the part size (about a texel) are left. A slide must not fold: a quad turned more than 78° against the surface under it, or against its original face (the fold check), pulls its vertices back halfway, up to four times, then they stay. Every vertex that should have slid but did not get all the way – pulled back, a boundary vertex (marked on the import, seams between shells stay closed), across a sharp edge (> 30°), onto a strongly curved facet (vertex normals spread > 8°: the flat facet of a coarse copy lies below the rounding, studs at level 1), onto another shell (a stud's fillet onto the plate beneath), or further than 1.5× its distance to the import point – gets **surface-fixed UVs** instead: the UV the import carries at the point nearest to the vertex, within the UV island of its original face. Corner normals of moved vertices: the smooth copy's normals at the new point, same side of a sharp edge. The shape is unchanged (points only slide on the surface); W14's smoothing stays the fallback if the slide fails (`rbcore/reparam.py`). **Rules 3.16:** the copy's points on an outline W21 closed do not slide (the cage's points of those edges and the 2^level − 1 points on each); and where the shrink compensation is undone on an island, W14c's marks are made again on the places the undo gave back. | The smoothing slides faces along the part, and with linear UVs every texture slides with them: 7052's mould line sits on a face ring narrower than the slide (line lower, corner reshaped, maintainer report); no UV rule alone fixes it – at the line's import place there are faces of another island. Sliding alone folds geometry at convex roundings (catalog: 1,324 of 1,927 parts; Windscreen L2 11,260 quads), hence the guard, and pulled-back vertices with linear UVs showed wrong texture (head 3626 L3: 54 texels), hence the surface-fixed fallback. Texture shift, 99 % of the corners (texels, L2 / L3): 7052 3.6 / 3.4 (1.2.3: 24), 3044 1.2 / 1.0, head 3626 2.7 / 2.4 (1.2.3: 4 / 3); L2 folds as 1.2.3. Catalog 1,927 parts: 1,924 OK, the same 3 CHECK as 1.2.4 (before the guard: 1,324). Cost (whole scene, best of 3): Banana Mech 15.0 → 15.6 s (+4 %), Optimus 29.7 → 32.3 s (+8 %); file sizes unchanged. Rules 3.16: on 23443 a slid point left the closed round end – the small spot and the shading in the red circle of run 302; holding every chain point instead made dark dashes on 89678's hole rim. The marks held the compensated places of the first bake, so an island put back after a fold slid to its compensated places anyway (23443: 0.28 mm off the copy without compensation). | runs 212–222; 303 |
| W15 | **Designed corners:** a crease-chain vertex (two chain edges) turning ≥ 20° (DESIGN_TURN) gets a vertex crease when its two chain edges differ in length ≥ 4× (DESIGN_RATIO) and the short edge ends at a junction of ≥ 3 creased edges – also on organic spots (W4: 60°), there only on parts with ≥ 12 % of the area in not curved islands (MECH_FLAT). **W15b:** an uncreased edge bending ≥ 20° between such a corner and another pinned corner is creased too. Arcs (K3/K3b) and groove ends stay free. | Tire 2515 (SuperStarDestroyer, user): the groove ends turn by 27° / 36° (edges 11.2 : 1.2, 10.6 : 2.0) on the tread, which counts as organic – rounded off; the real part (BrickLink photo, user photo) has sharp step ends. | W16 | **Coarse arcs across a flat face:** W3 is lifted at an outline vertex of a flat island that lies on one crease curve (exactly two other creased edges), bends 0.5° to < 30° (SHARP_TURN), touches a curved island and has a neighbour on the curve bending alike (bend directions within 60°): the island's inner edges at that vertex stay uncreased. | An inner edge creased by W3 that ends on the outline makes the outline vertex a corner (3+ creased edges) – the curve where a rounding meets the plane stays a polyline. Curved slopes 29119 / 29120 (user report): the curved top meets the 45° face with turns of 3.8° / 4.9° (below ROUND_TURN, so the face is "flat", not "flat-round") and the flat side with 6.5° / 6.4°; now even curves (outline turns 0 · 0 · 3.8 · 1.1 · 0 → 0.5 · 0.9 · 1.1 · 1.1 · 0.8 · 0.6). Bricks-Bows 58 / 115, Animals 37 / 135, Beams 0 / 19 parts change; no folds; deviation at most +0.013 (54091). | run 206 |
| W17 | **Coarse roundings:** a crease-chain vertex (two chain edges) that bends more than 30° (SHARP_TURN) and at most 60° (ORGANIC_CORNER) gets no vertex crease when it touches a curved island, its two chain edges differ in length by at most 2× (ROUNDING_RATIO), the creased line goes on at both neighbours with a bend of 0.5° to < 30° the same way (bend directions within 60°; at a junction along the creased edge that turns least), the three bends make at most 100° (ROUNDING_TOTAL), and the free vertex gives way by at most 0.06 mm (ROUNDING_DEPTH: \|a + b − 2v\| / 6, the limit of a creased line – a corner radius up to about 0.6 mm). On a flat face W3's inner edges end in the vertices of the rounding and make each a corner of three creases: on 18747 the back of the plate was round, the stud side kept two straight pieces at both lower corners (user report: "do you check both sides?"). | W18 | **Ends of coarse roundings:** where the wall of a rounding W17 has freed meets the next wall in a creased edge (a seam across the wall: its two faces bend 0.5° to < 30°), and an end of that seam carries just three creased edges – the rim of the rounding on the wall's face, the rim going on on the next wall's face at a bend of 0.5° to < 30°, and the seam – that end is the end of the rounding: the seam loses its crease and its hard edge (the wall's corners take the next wall's normal along it), the end loses its corner, and the next wall gets a **support edge** one piece of the rounding from the seam: a vertex on each of its two edges there (the next vertex of the edge if it is no further than 2 pieces, ROUNDING_RATIO), the face cut from one to the other. The seam's other end, if it is no such end, is pinned and moved by what the free end gives way, so the seam stays level. Not when the end would give way by more than 0.06 mm (ROUNDING_DEPTH), the next wall's face is not convex, or the render copy folds with it – then the part is welded again without W18. The cage then has other faces than the import: a copy carries `rb_layout` (faces with other corners, faces cut off and the import face they belong to), which the fold check, the T gaps, the texture placement (W14c) and the bevel's edge choice read (`checks.layout_of`, `checks.cage_faces`). | The two ends of a rounding can be modelled differently: on 18747 (user report: "only the lower side is subdivided, not the upper") the side has no edge across the wall and a vertex 0.29 mm on – the rounding runs out softly; the top has a hard edge across the wall and no vertex on the rim for 2 mm – the end is a corner with three creases and the outline kept a kink of 21.8° at every level (level 1: 23.1°). Releasing the seam alone moves the kink (the end slides 0.28 mm along the rim; level 1: 45°); a vertex on the rim alone changes nothing (the end stays a corner); both, on the rim only, leave the seam to the middle of the next wall – the surface gives way most between the rims (0.15 on walls 6 to 9 mm wide, two parts folded). With the support edge the outline runs out as at the side: level 2 kinks 9.8 · 8.0 · 6.5 · 5.7 · 4.7 · 2.4 · 0.8, level 1 16.1 · 11.5 · 4.9; the end gives way by 0.019. Only at roundings W17 freed: the same pattern at a vertex of a polygonised arc (18747: the outline of the body where it meets this seam) is no rounded corner. Library (4,776 parts): 105 of W17's 234 parts, 532 seams, 961 vertices; folds, seam gaps, repair passes unchanged; taken back on 2 parts (con1, con1uv: two folding faces). The numbers of both rules together stand at W17. | run 283 |
| W19 | **Shrink compensation of whole cylinders** (from rules 3.16 the first of W19, W22, W23, W24 – what it moves is done, the others build on its places; only the points W25b / W25c hold go back)**:** a smooth region of the cage (faces joined by edges that are not fully creased) that is a whole cylinder – every face normal square to one axis (within cos 0.1), its points all round the axis (no gap over 45°) and at one distance (within 3 % of the radius) – is widened square to its axis until the smooth surface has the import's distance from the axis again (the subdivision keeps the cage's point numbers: `subdiv_foreach.cc`, `vertices_corner_offset`); up to 6 rounds of 70 %, the best kept, no point further than 1.5 times its own shrink, only shrink corrected. | W20 | **Per island, and large meshes in batches:** every decision of the rules is taken per island of the welded cage, never for the whole mesh - the rounds of the shrink compensation (W19) and its undo after a fold, W18 left out after a fold, the slide's nearest point (W14c: on the vertex's own shell, of triangles at the same distance the one facing the vertex, then the flattest, then by place; surface-fixed UVs from the corner's own face first), the part size that scales the slide (the import's), and every step of *Edges: Bevel* (support edges, normals, net, profile: the edge's own island; islands without a chosen edge stay as baked). A unit equal to an earlier one moved (corners, positions to 0.0001 mm, every attribute the rules read) takes that one's copy at its place; face attributes the rules do not read (stud numbers) stay its own; a unit whose copy has W18 faces or T points is baked itself. Bake quality and flat share (W15) are those of the whole mesh. | The 48 x 48 baseplate 4186 needed about 58 GB and stopped (the library run 285 lost Plates and Plates-Baseplates the same way); in batches 12.3 GB, with shared shapes (6 shapes of 11,521 units) 6.2 GB and about half a minute (3857: 8 s against 60 s direct; the 512 equal studs of the direct bake differ among themselves by 0.0066 in the normals - the shared copy lies within that). ## 1 · How Mecabricks builds parts (observed)

- Faces are split into **loose islands along hard edges**; hard edges are open bound­aries, not creases.
- All faces are **flat-flagged**; the look comes from **custom normals**.
- Circles are **regular poly­gons** (12, 16, 32, 64 seg­ments).
- Stud logos are an **embossed layer** (height ≈ 0.12, sloped flanks ~68°) with **open slits** at inner corners (bottom vertex dupli­cated).
- Axes differ per part (3001: Y up; 3648: axle along Z) – no rule may assume an axis or a size.

## 4 · T fans, self-check and fold repair

**F0 · T fans (before the fold loop).** Every T fan in a flat island (arc vertex with three inte­rior edges: a strip in the middle, two diag­o­nals to the strip's corners) is eval­u­ated at diag­o­nal creases 0, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0 (mir­rored onto the seam partner, T free). A fan is creased only if it **folds** at 0 or if the crease **reduces** how far its sub-edges cross the straight corner outline (depth at 0 > depth at 1.0 + 0.005). A fan that needs it gets its diag­o­nals creased **fully (1.0)**; the T group is pinned by the seam rule. Cross­ings that do not react to the crease come from else­where and are left alone.
Why not a partial crease (runs 17–18): it would have to be mir­rored onto the partner's edge, whose far end lies on the next ring – the crease runs on as a **chain of points** through the whole hole (48168: 0.051 at every ring, run 20). A pin does not travel along edges: one point at the T, the rings stay round.
Origin: 48452 upper T – no fold, but a 0.054 sliver at the rib corner, clean at 0.3; 48168 lower T – folds, clean at 0.4; 3648 gear – two fans with 0.035 slivers, clean at 0.4; other gear fans cross by a con­stant 0.025/0.081 unre­lated to the fan (run 18).


After the crease rules the part is sub­di­vided and every orig­i­nal face is com­pared with its sub-faces. A sub-face turned by more than 90° is a **fold**. Folding islands are repaired step by step, gen­tlest first; the check repeats (max. 7 passes).

| Step | Repair | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **T fan** still folding after F0: diagonal from the T vertex at 0.4, mirrored (R8), T free. **Other folds:** crease the interior edges of the folding faces at **0.5**. | Pinning vertices never stops a fold, creasing the right edge does (run 14). Diagonal-only scan (run 17): 0.2 stops the flip but the sub-faces still cross the inside corner (visible sliver); 0.4 is the lowest clean value, point at the T 0.051 instead of 0.079 |
| F2 | Crease the interior edges of the folding faces **and their neighbours** at 1.0. | Run 12 |
| F3 | Crease **all** edges of those faces and neighbours. | Run 12 |
| F4 | flat-round only: crease the **whole island** (last resort). | Run 11 |

Why folds happen (turntable, runs 16–17): the new point on an inte­rior edge is the average of its ends and the centres of the two faces beside it. A long neigh­bour­ing face (e.g. a rib 3.05 long instead of 2.1) pulls that point past a nearby corner and the thin face beside it flips. A crease puts the point back on the middle of the edge.

### Edges: Bevel (add-on 1.3.0, runs 273 and 274)

With *Edges: Bevel* the copies get rounded edges of their own in place of the importer's bevel shader. Which edges: a fully creased edge of the welded cage that was a border between two islands of the import, or an edge inside one island that bends by 85 degrees or more (W12). Left out: the logos, and the outline of a round­ing Mecabricks mod­elled already (W13 – rim and foot of a stud). On 3004: 368 fully creased edges, 47 bev­elled, 251 logo, 70 modelled. Also left out: the two borders of a chamfer Mecabricks mod­elled (a face nar­rower than 1.5 mm between two edges that both bend by less than 60 degrees). This is decided for a whole run of chosen edges – edges joined where a point has exactly two of them (run 290): a run is a chamfer's border when such edges make half of its length or more, and then all of it; oth­er­wise none of it. On organic parts narrow faces come and go along a fold, and an edge left out here and there ended the bevel in the middle of the fold (the hair 11256, user report: 97 such points, none now). Library (4,776 parts): 864 parts change, nearly all organic (hair, head wear, animals, acces­sories); runs on bricks are a chamfer's border as a whole or not at all and stay as they were. The choice is written as bevel weight on the cage and sur­vives the sub­di­vi­sion; the fin­ished copy, checked as always, is rounded and replaced by the result. The rules them­selves and their version are unchanged: the bevel is a prop­erty of the copy, like its level.

Every chosen edge is rounded at one size, in the shading of the copy; its outline is not changed (runs 277 to 281).

- **The round­ing is in the normals.** Every corner closer to a chosen edge than the width is turned towards the other side of the edge: by half the angle between the two sides on the edge itself, in a straight line down to nothing at the width. That is how the normal runs across a bevel of one segment. The width is the panel's Radius, the edge radius in mil­lime­tres; the shader's Bevel value that looks the same size is the Radius divided by 0.595 (run 275). A corner near several edges takes the turn of each, no corner more than 56 degrees.
- **Support edges give it its size.** A normal can only change from one point of the mesh to the next; where the next point beside an edge lies mil­lime­tres away, the round­ing would run across all of that face (streaks). So every face that reaches from inside the round­ing to outside it is cut where the round­ing ends: per run of chosen edges (a straight or gently bent piece, ended by a bend of more than 50 degrees), the edges of the face that cross the line at one width from the run get a point there and the points are joined. Runs are done one after the other, twice round, so their borders cross each other like any other edge. Nothing is moved.
- **The net.** A face with a turned corner that still reaches further than 1.5 widths from every chosen edge gets its plain normals back. The copy records how many (`rb_bevel_plain`).
- Cutting faces does not keep custom normals, so all normals of the copy are set anew from the copy before the cuts.
- A bevel of faces (Blender's Bevel mod­i­fier) was built first and is out: after the sub­di­vi­sion most edges have no room for it; at part of the width it turned faces over (on the part 78301, 1641 of 1642 turned faces lay at such a bevel), at the full width only where a whole edge has room it still turned 8 to 132 faces per copy in the trial of run 280, and where it stopped in mid edge it left slits.

- **Tangent point of a rounded corner (run 42, part.375 = 36840.004):** where an arc of one island ends at a real corner of a neigh­bour­ing flat island (a straight side face), the shared point stays pinned; the arc's last turn (e.g. 15°) remains as a small kink instead of a tangent blend. Freeing it would bend the flat face and open the seam – not solv­able without chang­ing the mesh (P1).
- **Polyg­o­nal holes with split walls (run 44, horse nostril 10350):** a hole whose walls are sep­a­rate flat faces meeting at the corners keeps its polygon – the corner pins of the walls hold the rim of the sur­round­ing face; round­ing the rim alone would open gaps.

3. Verify: `verify_part.py -- <import.blend>` – all checks in section 6.
4. Render: `render_generic.py`, plus close-ups of any­thing unusual.
5. A failure is a finding: find the cause, adjust or add a rule here, rerun **all** tested parts, then record it in the journal.
